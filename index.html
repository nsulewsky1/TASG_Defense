The TASG model

Find the gap. Shape the path. Build the right team.

TASG focuses on the earliest—and often most neglected—part of capability development: defining the operational need clearly enough that engineers, funders, manufacturers, and acquirers can move with purpose.

01 / Identify

Start with a real problem.

Focus on repeated, consequential user problems observed through operational experience, field context, and practical employment analysis.

02 / Architect

Translate need into a concept path.

Build requirements, interfaces, user burden assumptions, documentation, risk questions, and evidence needs without claiming premature maturity.

03 / Transition

Align the organizations that can finish it.

Bring in the engineering, prototype, test, manufacturing, funding, and strategic partners needed to turn disciplined concepts into credible opportunities.

Development path

Serious concepts need honest gates.

TASG is designed to be direct about what is established, what is active, and what still requires funding, technical validation, or industrial support.

01 / Established

Operational gap defined

The user problem and operational consequence are clear enough to justify concept development.

02 / Active

Concept architecture

Requirements, employment logic, development boundaries, and partner pathway are being refined.

03 / Next gate

Engineering feasibility

Mechanical, packaging, manufacturability, and test questions should be explored through disciplined prototype planning.

04 / Planned

Funded prototype

Initial fabrication, controlled evaluation, and data collection should occur only after the right support is in place.

05 / Future gate

Industrial transition

Transition should align with organizations capable of integration, scale, certification, and acquisition execution.
